# Prediction markets: surging volume, thin consumer price protection

Research for the problem slide (slide 2). Compiled 7 Oct 2026; every figure carries its date and
source. "Notional" volume counts each contract at its $1 payout; the dollars customers actually pay
are much lower (about a quarter on Kalshi, see 1.1). Notional is therefore not directly comparable
with sportsbook handle, and we say so wherever we compare them.

## 1. Volume is surging

**1.1 Kalshi and Polymarket**

- Combined monthly volume on Kalshi and Polymarket **more than doubled from $26B (May 2026) to
  $53B (July 2026)**, mostly sports; in June–July (FIFA World Cup) sports topped $58B on Kalshi
  and neared $22B on Polymarket. Kalshi's July sports volume was $31.4B, about three-quarters of
  its month. Pew Research Center, 23 Sep 2026, using data from The Block:
  <https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/23/prediction-markets-trading-volume-doubled-between-may-and-july-largely-driven-by-sports/>
- **Kalshi, September 2026: a record $60.85B notional**, up 52% on August ($40.03B). Combos
  (parlays) were 58.3% ($35.5B), standalone sports 24.6% ($15.0B) and crypto 14.6%. NFL was $2.22B.
  Source: NEXTPredict, from TickerTracker data, Oct 2026:
  <https://nextpredict.io/market-news/industry/kalshi-60bn-september-parlays-taker-volume-lags/>
- **Year-on-year growth of about 46×:** Kalshi's average daily notional was $28.2M in August 2025
  and $1.29B in August 2026 (NEXTPredict, Sep 2026:
  <https://nextpredict.io/market-news/industry/kalshi-tops-40bn-volume-parlays-drive-growth/>).
- **Notional vs cash:** over 5 Sep – 4 Oct 2026 Kalshi traded $66.3B in contracts but $15.8B in
  dollars paid, 78% of all prediction-market volume (DeFi Rate dashboard, read 7 Oct 2026:
  <https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/volume/kalshi/>).
- **Polymarket** peaked at $11.3B in June 2026 (DeFi Rate:
  <https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/volume/polymarket/>). It first passed $10B a month in
  March 2026, $10.57B (BitKE, Apr 2026: <https://bitcoinke.io/2026/04/polymarket-in-march-2026/>).
  Its US unit grew from 4% of Polymarket volume in January to 39% in July 2026 (Pew, above).

**1.3 Comparison with legal sportsbooks (AGA)**

- US sportsbook **handle was $38.84B in Q2 2026** (+7.8% YoY) at a 10.1% hold. July 2026 handle was
  $11.60B (+31.9%) (AGA Commercial Gaming Revenue Tracker, released 26 Aug and 24 Sep 2026:
  <https://www.americangaming.org/resources/commercial-gaming-revenue-tracker/>).
- So Kalshi's July 2026 *sports* notional ($31.4B) was about 2.7× all US sportsbook handle
  ($11.6B) that month. On cash paid, roughly a quarter of notional, the two are of similar
  magnitude. This is our calculation from the two sources, so treat it as approximate.

## 2. Regulatory status

- **Kalshi** has been a CFTC designated contract market (DCM) since November 2020. It
  **self-certified** its "Will win?" sports contracts on 22 Jan 2025 and began listing them on
  24 Jan 2025 (CFTC filing: <https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/filings/ptc/25/01/ptc01222514045.pdf>).
  The CFTC has the power to review "gaming" contracts as contrary to the public interest
  (7 U.S.C. §7a-2(c)(5)(C); 17 C.F.R. §40.11) but has not used it against sports contracts.
  The Third Circuit noted this in April 2026 (*KalshiEX v. Flaherty*:
  <https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-3rd-circuit/118272839.html>).
- **CFTC rulemaking:** an advance notice of proposed rulemaking on prediction markets
  (91 Fed. Reg. 12516, 16 Mar 2026: <https://www.cftc.gov/LawRegulation/FederalRegister/proposedrules/2026-05105.html>)
  asked about inside information and manipulation. A proposed rule defining "gaming" followed on
  12 Jun 2026 (<https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-12/html/2026-11854.htm>). Neither
  is final.
- **States and courts, as of 7 Oct 2026, are split:**
  - The **Third Circuit** (6 Apr 2026, 2–1) held that New Jersey likely cannot regulate Kalshi's
    sports contracts (Reuters:
    <https://www.reuters.com/world/new-jersey-cannot-regulate-kalshis-prediction-market-us-appeals-court-rules-2026-04-06/>).
  - The **Ninth Circuit** (28 Aug 2026, 3–0) held that Nevada *can* (opinion:
    <https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/25-7516/25-7516-2026-08-28.pdf>;
    Reuters: <https://www.reuters.com/world/kalshi-cannot-block-nevada-oversight-sports-prediction-markets-us-appeals-court-2026-08-28/>).
  - The Fourth Circuit (Maryland, argued 7 May) and Sixth Circuit (Ohio and Tennessee, argued
    30 Jul) have not ruled. New Jersey has petitioned the Supreme Court. Nevada, Michigan and
    Washington currently enforce orders restricting Kalshi, and the Massachusetts injunction is
    stayed (Finance Magnates, Sep 2026:
    <https://www.financemagnates.com/fintech/federal-courts-now-disagree-on-kalshis-sports-contracts-new-jersey-wants-the-supreme-court-to-settle-the-fight/>).
  - The Arizona attorney general filed criminal charges against Kalshi in March 2026 (Axios,
    23 Mar 2026: <https://www.axios.com/2026/03/23/kalshi-prediction-markets-insider-trading-ban>).
- **Polymarket** settled with the CFTC in 2022 ($1.4M penalty; agreed to block US users) (The
  Block: <https://www.theblock.co/post/399234/polymarket-eyes-return-main-exchange-us-in-talks-cftc-bloomberg>).
  It re-entered the US by buying QCEX, a CFTC-licensed exchange and clearinghouse, for $112M
  (21 Jul 2025: <https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/polymarket-acquires-cftc-licensed-exchange-and-clearinghouse-qcex-for-112-million-302509626.html>).
  The CFTC's **amended order of designation of 24 Nov 2025** allows broker-intermediated US
  trading as Polymarket US (<https://www.cftc.gov/media/12806/Polymarket%2520US%2520Amended%2520Order%2520of%2520Designation/download>).
  The global exchange remains offshore.

## 3. The prices consumers are offered

- **No house edge, but a cost per trade.** Kalshi's taker fee is
  `round up(0.07 × C × P × (1 − P))` dollars (C contracts at price P). It peaks at 1.75¢ per
  contract at 50¢, and resting (maker) orders pay 0.0175 × C × P × (1 − P) on listed series,
  including most sports (Kalshi fee schedule, Jul 2026:
  <https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf>). Retail market orders also cross the bid-ask
  spread. **Our measurement** on 1,316 NBA games: a $20 order one hour before tip pays 1.92¢ per
  contract on average (0.53¢ half-spread + 1.39¢ fee), **about 5% of the price** and 9–14% on
  contracts under 20¢; and 91% of the news-driven price move happens before the public inactive list
  (`evaluation/results/consumer_fairness.md`).
- **Professional counterparties, including the exchange's affiliate.** Kalshi Trading LLC, an
  affiliate of the exchange, has traded on Kalshi since June 2021 as "a profit-making enterprise
  to help support liquidity" (Kalshi notice, 21 Sep 2021:
  <https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/Kalshi+Text+of+Website+Notice+of+Affiliate+Entity+Member+09.21.21.pdf>).
  The CFTC has proposed affiliate-trading rules, warning that "the exchange is asked to
  investigate and, if warranted, discipline the source of its own revenue". Kalshi says its
  affiliate was about 1.5% of 2026 volume and has offered a 5% quarterly cap (Sportico, 2026:
  <https://www.sportico.com/business/sports-betting/2026/prediction-market-affiliate-rule-cftc-conflicts-trading-1234941260/>,
  <https://www.sportico.com/business/sports-betting/2026/kalshi-trading-affiliate-cftc-comment-rule-1234946760/>).
  Robinhood's Rothera is a joint venture with the market maker Susquehanna (1.4).
- **What the CFTC regime protects.** The DCM core principles require surveillance, prevention of
  manipulation, conflict-of-interest procedures and fair trading on a central limit order book.
  That covers *market integrity*: everyone trades at the same displayed prices. Exchange rules
  impose no obligation that the price a retail user receives is *fair* relative to the event's
  probability, and we found no best-execution duty for a consumer trading directly on a DCM.
  This is our reading of the core principles, not a legal opinion.
- **Insider trading** is banned by exchange rules and by CEA §6(c)(1) / Reg. 180.1. Kalshi's first
  public disciplinary cases (25 Feb 2026) were a MrBeast video editor ($20,397.58 penalty and a
  2-year suspension) and a gubernatorial candidate (CFTC advisory:
  <https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9185-26>). From March 2026 Kalshi pre-screens
  athletes, coaches and officials with IC360 (Axios, above; <https://kalshi.com/policy-center/insider-trading>).
- **Gambling-harm protections.** State-licensed sportsbooks are generally 21+ and must offer
  responsible-gambling tools, self-exclusion and advertising limits. Sports contracts on DCMs are
  open at **18+**. The NBA asked the CFTC on 30 Apr 2026 to raise this to 21 and to require
  responsible-trading tools (Covers, 1 May 2026:
  <https://www.covers.com/industry/nba-calls-for-prediction-market-regulatory-framework-in-letter-to-cftc-may-1-2026>).
  The CFTC's proposals so far set **no federal age floor** and no responsible-gambling obligations
  (June 2026 proposed rule, above).
- **Tax** treatment differs (gambling winnings vs Section 1256 / derivatives treatment), but we
  did not verify a current authoritative source, so it stays off the slides.

## 4. The gap: where consumers fall between the two regimes

| Protection | State sportsbook | CFTC exchange (Kalshi, Polymarket US) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pricing | House sets odds with a built-in margin (hold 10.1%, Q2 2026); disclosed to the regulator | No house edge, but fees + spread; professional and affiliate market makers |
| Fair-price oversight | State approves wager types and house rules | Integrity and manipulation surveillance only; no fair-price or best-execution duty |
| Minimum age | 21 in most states | 18 |
| Gambling harm | Self-exclusion, RG tools, ad limits (state law) | None required federally |
| Who regulates | State gaming board | CFTC; states' authority contested (3rd vs 9th Cir.) |

**Bottom line.** Volume is growing many times faster than state-regulated sportsbooks, and it is
sold to millions of retail users through brokerage apps. Those users get exchange-style price
protection (equal access to a public order book), but no one checks whether the prices they pay
leave a realistic chance of profit. Our measured 5% cost per trade, and the 91% of the move that
happens before public news, are exactly the costs this gap leaves unaddressed. They also get none
of the gambling-harm protections that a 21+ state sportsbook must provide.
